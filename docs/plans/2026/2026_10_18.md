# Creatures as Wild Ghosts

## What changed since 2026-10-17

- **The human-reserved `src/core` is dropped.** The user found that "trying to make two completely segregated parts of the codebase communicate with each other feels a bit awkward". Claude implements the feature alone; the user contributes ideas, not necessarily implementation details.
  - Removed today: `src/core`, the guard hooks, the rule page, the boundary test and their mentions.
- **Creatures are no longer pets owned by users.** Owned creatures travelling with their owner were costly either way:
  - updated in real time, they risked performance, race conditions and other edge cases;
  - driven by intents alone, they could not interact in real time with fans' wind and other rigidbodies.

## The idea (the user's)

1. **Ownerless, server-side creatures.** The server spawns and destroys them at random moments, and they exist without any user present for as long as the server runs. They are saved in the DB and resume their states when the server shuts down and recovers.
2. **"Wild animals" or "ghosts", not pets.** The server has no real-time physics (no `Rigidbody`), so their movement is based on intents rather than real-time position and direction changes. Their only physical interaction with the room is rudimentary pathfinding around voxel blocks.
   - As ghosts, passing through a block now and then is fine: pathfinding is inaccurate at times, or clients lag behind.
   - Ignoring blocks altogether is not.

## Outline

- **Object.** A new `Creature` object type, with its own category and a per-room cap, which also sizes every client's mesh pools.
  - It is non-persistent: never in `ObjectGroup` saves or room files, and its moves never mark the room dirty.
  - It is owned by no user. Only the server adds, removes or changes one, through `ServerObjectManager.addObjectAsServer` / `removeObjectAsServer` (from the 10-17 Player fix).
  - Room snapshots already carry non-persistent objects to joiners. A user leaving never removes a creature, since it is nobody's.
- **Where.** Multiplayer rooms only: hubs and Regular rooms. Single-player rooms hold no server state, so they have none.
- **Server host** (`ServerCreatureManager`): one per loaded room, handling population, decisions, paths and broadcasts.
  - It runs from the 200 ms flush callback, just before the flush, and never at module load, because `MODE=ssg` runs the same bundle.
  - Each tick has a budget, spent over a queue ordered by each creature's next decision time.
  - The event-loop delay log (added 10-17) is the before/after measure.
- **Population.** Each room has a target count by room type, under the cap.
  - A creature spawns at a random moment in a random open cell, and vanishes after a random lifetime.
  - Appearing and vanishing are domain events, which VFX bind to.
- **Behaviour** (The Sims, steered only indirectly):
  - Needs change over time, starting with hunger and fun, the two named in the user's `CreatureNeed` on 10-18.
  - Props and canvases advertise what they satisfy, through a curated map keyed by picture path, plus defaults per object type.
  - A creature picks by need, distance and a little randomness, floats there, uses the thing for a while and has the need restored. Otherwise it wanders.
  - The server alone decides, so two creatures wanting one TV are settled by a claim the server holds.
- **Movement.** Ghosts float.
  - A path is a list of open cells, found by A* over the cell grid in three dimensions and smoothed by line of sight, so storeys and stairs need no special rules.
  - No physics: fans do not push them, and players pass through them.
  - Paths are found per decision, never per tick. A block placed across a path afterwards is passed through until the next decision.
- **Sync.** One message per decision: the path from where the creature is, its speed and its activity.
  - The server sets it as a server-only metadata key on the creature, and it rides on the 200 ms flush. Every client animates the creature along it alike.
  - The server works out where a creature is along its path from the time elapsed. It keeps the creature's stored transform current, so a joiner's snapshot shows it in the right place with the rest of its path.
  - No shared clock is needed.
- **Persistence.** Creature state is saved with its room at the room's save points (autosave, unload, shutdown), in a versioned section outside `ObjectGroup`, so room files never carry it. It is restored on load.
  - A crash loses what happened since the last save.
  - Regular rooms unload when empty, so their creatures pause until the room loads again. Hubs stay loaded.
- **Client.** A `CreatureGameObject` with a component that follows the path. For v1 the look is the player's body codec, drawn small and ghostly.
- **Users.** They steer creatures only through what they place, and nobody owns one. An admin's debug commands spawn and clear creatures for testing, and creatures stay admin-only until the feature is finished.

## Costs

- **Server CPU.** Decisions and A* run in every loaded room with creatures, including hubs with nobody in them. Measure before turning it on:
  - an esbuild benchmark of decisions and paths;
  - the staging event-loop delay with creatures on and off.
- **Bandwidth.** One path per decision per creature, multicast, far below a stream of positions.
- **DB.** Nothing extra: the creature section rides on saves the room makes anyway.

## Open questions

- The look: translucent mini player bodies, or a ghost of its own?
- Do empty loaded rooms (hubs) keep simulating, or pause until someone arrives? Nobody would see the difference, and pausing saves CPU.
- Population per room, and whether an owner may turn creatures off in their own room. That would be a room-level parameter, which room generation then has to decide.
- Any direct interaction, such as clicking a creature?

## Milestones

1. The object type, the server's spawner, and the client's path follower, with creatures floating in straight lines and an admin spawn command.
2. Pathfinding and the path message, with wandering.
3. Needs, the picture-path map, use spots and claims.
4. Saving and restoring with the room.
5. Population tuning, the look, VFX and a docs page. Turned on for everyone after measuring on staging.
