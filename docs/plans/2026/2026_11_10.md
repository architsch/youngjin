# Cannons

Let's implement a new type of object called "Cannon". Here are the details:

## Placement and Configuration

- Any type of user can add a cannon to the room, but the user must pay $10 each time he adds it (The "add cannon" button will need to display the cost indicator UI near its below it, which means the whole voxelPlacementOptions menu bar will need to have some margin (with background) below its row of buttons to make enough space for cost indicators). If the user has fewer than 10 dollars (i.e. doesn't satisfy the cost constraint), the "add cannon" button must be disabled.

- Each cannon should occupy a chunk of space that is of size 1x1x1 in world-space coordinates (i.e. a unit cube); this cube-shaped chunk's bottom side should be attached to a surface of voxel blocks, just like a canvas, fan, lamp, or prop. The actual hitbox, though, will need to be slightly shrunk in order to prevent it from overlapping with adjacent voxel blocks & attached objects.

- The user should be able to attach a cannon on a floor or wall but not on a ceiling.

- A cannon's "(...)EditOptions" UI will need two options:
    - A "remove" button to let the user remove the cannon prematurely (before it consumes all 20 of its stored cannon balls).
    - An on/off toggle switch to let the user temporarily deactivate the cannon and then resume its aiming/firing activity later on. While deactivated, the cannon should look down and show a small "z z z" (sleeping) VFX rising above it.

## Behaviors

- A cannon, when either newly installed or activated (from its OFF state), should just rest for a bit (for about 2 seconds) before doing anything.

- The cannon, at each random time interval after the beginning of its lifetime (Each interval is randomly chosen between 6 seconds and 10 seconds, inclusive), rotates its main body to the center of the nearest player's hitbox, and shoots a cannon ball toward that center after a very brief post-rotation delay which is about 0.5 seconds or so. A cannon must NOT shoot a cannon ball either if the nearest player is too far away (more than 16 units away), or if the ray from the nearest player's center to the cannon's cannon ball spawn location is blocked by an obstacle, or if the nearest player is way too close to the cannon (within 2 units or so). Also, during the brief post-rotation delay, the cannon shouldn't care if the player moved during this short time gap. Once the cannon is done aiming at the player's location and begins its "being ready to fire" period, it should just keep that particular aim and then fire in that direction. This also means that the cannon shouldn't be continuously adjusting its angle in order to aim itself at the player with prefect precision; just stop rotating and begin the firing process once the aim becomes "close enough".

- The cannon can only fire up to 20 cannon balls. Once it uses up all 20 cannon balls, the cannon destroys itself (with a smooth destruction sound and a brief "puff" particle effect).

- The cannon ball must move linearly (without being influenced by gravity), although its trajectory can be changed along the way due to the presence of a fan nearby (A fan should "push" any cannon ball which passes in front of it). The cannon ball's initial velocity should be quite slow (Slow enough for the player to dodge easily), which may be about 3 units in world-space per second.

- When the cannon ball hits somewhere, we will suppose that there will be a spherical volume of explosion of radius 0.5 which is centered at the ball's hit point (lasting for only a single frame of time). Then, we will find all colliders which happen to intersect this spherical volume, and iterate through each of these colliders. For each collider in the iteration:
    - If the collider belongs to any rigidbody (such as a player), destroy the cannon ball and apply a strong "push" to any rigidbody in the radial direction with respect to the cannon ball's center, as well as a strong upward force (in the global coordinate system) to briefly lift the rigidbody a bit in a bouncy manner while it is being pushed away (so as to make the push feel more dramatic).
    - If the collider belongs to another cannon ball, make the two cannon balls bounce against each other (like two billiard balls) instead of necessarily destroying themselves.
    - If the collider belongs to the client's own player (i.e. the one with the first-person camera) and the client's GameMode is currently set to "play" mode, the first-person camera should shake a bit very briefly in order to convey the sense of being directly "hit" by the cannon ball.
    - If the collider belongs to a voxel block which does NOT belong to a restricted zone (see @docs/gameplay/restricted_zone.md ):
        - If every one of the attachable objects (see @docs/geometry/object_attachment.md ), which are at least partially attached to the block, is one which can be added by any type of user in the edit-mode, destroy the cannon ball as well as both the voxel block and every one of its attached objects.
        - If at least one of the attachable objects, which are at least partially attached to the block, is one which can only be added by either the admin or superuser (e.g. room's owner), destroy the cannon ball but destroy NEITHER the voxel block nor any of its attached objects.
    - If the collider belongs to a voxel block which DOES belong to a restricted zone, just destroy the cannon ball without destroying anything else.

- When the cannon ball explodes and its spherical volume of explosion happens to intersect at least one of the player hitboxes, it must NOT drop any item when being destroyed. If no player was hit, on the other hand, the ball must drop a dollar bill when being destroyed. This way, the player will have a good reason to avoid a cannon ball that is about to hit him (i.e. "Avoid being hit by the ball, and you will get a dollar which you will be able to pick up!"). By the way, each dollar bill that is spawned by a cannon ball should count toward the total number of bills within the room, and the room shouldn't randomly spawn any new bill as long as there are 2 or more bills inside the room in total.

- When the cannon ball flies out of the room, it should pass through the room's boundary colliders without being hit by them and continue moving linearly until it gets sufficiently far away from the room's boundary (e.g. at least 20 world units away from the boundary or so). Then, the cannon ball should silently be removed (no SFX or VFX) without dropping any item.

# Mesh

- The cannon mesh, for performance reasons, should consist of a number of InstancedTin & InstancedEmissive mesh instances (just like PlayerGameObject) such as the cannon's main body (silver cylinder) which can be rotated to face the target at which the cannon is aiming (+ a thin, disk-like dark monotonic (InstancedEmissive) cylinder to express the hole through which the cannon ball is to come out), a thin rusty brown square plate at the very bottom, and a cuboid pillar which sits between the square plate and the cannon's main cylindrical body. On each of the 4 XZ-facing sides of the cuboid pillar will have a thin vertical slider (made up of two InstancedEmissive squares that are stretched vertically) which comprises the dark background part and the light green foreground part. The slider must be full initially, which means that the cannon has 20 cannon balls in it. Each time a ball gets fired, the slider ticks down by 1/20 of its full scale (since 1 out of 20 balls would be used up). Once the slider hits the bottom (meaning that the cannon used up all 20 of its stored balls), the cannon will destroy itself.

- The cannon ball mesh must be a near-black (very dark gray) InstancedTin sphere, which is of radius 0.25 and has a collider of a spherical shape and the same radius (This will require some adjustment in the physics system to let it handle not only 3D AABBs but also spheres for collision). Make sure to make this sphere low-poly so as to keep the performance high.

## Effects

- There should be SFX and VFX instances for the following events:
    - A brief firing SFX and VFX when the cannon fires a cannon ball.
    - A brief explosion SFX and VFX when the cannon ball hits somewhere and explodes.
    - A "puff" SFX and VFX when the cannon destroys itself after consuming all 20 of its stored cannon balls.
    - A small "z z z" (sleeping) VFX rising above each deactivated cannon.
    - A looping gear-grinding SFX (not so loud) while the cannon's main body is rotating toward the neareat player.
    - A central SFX instance and a multitude of destruction (debris) VFX instances which cover any attached object's hitbox, in case the object gets destroyed (for whichever reason, whether it be a manual edit-mode removal, a destruction of a voxel block to which part of its hitbox is attached, etc). Make sure that these effects are sufficienly moderate in their strength/scale, so as not to annoy the viewer too much whenever an object gets destroyed.
    - "cha-ching" (item-unlock) SFX instead of the usual object addition SFX when adding a new cannon to the room (because adding a cannon requires the user to make a payment).

## State Management

- Both the cannon's and cannon ball's states must be managed by the server and synced up across all clients (just like the NPC characters and collectible objects).
- The number of remaining cannon balls, the current aiming angle, on/off status, and the current time (phase) state of each cannon must persist across sessions and survive room reloading + server reboot. Make sure to use as few ObjectMetadataKeys as possible when managing these states (so as to keep the number of keys and the encoded data size as small as possible). Cannon balls, two, should preserve their states (position, velocity, etc).
