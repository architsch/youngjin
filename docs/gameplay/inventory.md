# Inventory and Dollar Bills

What a user holds, the game's measure of progress, and the $1 bills that fill it.

## Inventory
- A map from item type (`InventoryItemTypeEnumMap`, its values stored, so never renamed) to quantity, in `DBUser`. Guests and agents have one; signing up keeps it, being the same row.
- **Server**: read from the row on connecting and kept on the `UserContext` for the visit. Each gain reaches the row at once, as an atomic increment, so a crash loses nobody what they earned. It is written directly rather than through `DBQuery`, whose rewrite of a row being migrated would read a nested path as a field's name.
- **Client**: told all of it on connecting and on each change (`InventorySignal`). `DollarCounter` shows the count in the corner of a multiplayer room's screen. A dollar shows there only once its bill has flown to it (see Drawing), which is when it bounces and glows: `ClientInventoryManager` holds back those on their way.
- **Login reward**: coming back earns `LOGIN_REWARD_DOLLARS` once a day, on a clock of its own (`DBUser.lastLoginRewardAt`): due a little under a day after the last was given, so that coming at about the same time each day always finds it due, whatever visits came between. `loginCount` plays no part. A new row's clock starts as it is made, so a first login earns nothing, and a migrated row's at its last visit. Agents earn it alike.
  - Claimed as the user arrives (`ServerInventoryManager`), by a transaction on the row (`DBInventoryUtil`), so refreshes, reconnections and tabs give it once, and a failed claim comes again at the next connection. An admin's `fast_forward_daily_reward_clock` debug command makes it due for testing, checked by the server (`UserCommandUtil`).
  - `LoginRewardSignal` follows what they hold. A browser holds the dollars back until they can be seen, in a multiplayer room, past the loading screen, on a visible page (`LoginRewardUtil`). Then the count rises under a reward's coach mark (see [ftue.md](../networking/ftue.md)), both bouncing; the mark goes on its own.
- **Asking a guest to sign in** (`SignInPromptUtil`): once in a page's life, as its first dollar reaches a guest, by a bill or a login's reward, a coach mark under the count offers signing in, after any reward's mark has gone. It stays until closed. The count paints over coach marks, as this one stays through later bounces.
- **Buying a shape** (`PlayerShapeItemUtil`): each shape of a character's part past the free ones is an item for sale, held once bought, so it lives in the same row and map as the dollars paying for it. A user asks by `BuyItemSignal`, and hears the outcome by `BuyItemResultSignal`, after what they now hold.
  - **Both or neither**: one transaction on the row takes the dollars and gives the item (`DBInventoryUtil`), and its word is the one that counts; what the `UserContext` holds only spares it requests that can't succeed. The item is read in the transaction, so a repeated request pays nothing twice, and only items for sale can be asked for.
  - **Looks are held to it**: the server refuses a look with a locked shape, from a browser or an agent, like any refused edit, judging what browsers would draw from it (`ServerInventoryManager`). A shape already worn passes, so that a look from before can still change color. An NPC's look is an admin's, and nothing is locked in it.
  - **In a browser** nothing shows as spent or unlocked until the answer comes (`PlayerShapeUnlockUtil`). Meanwhile the tile is darkened under a spinner, and the screen takes no input (`ScreenInputBlockUtil`, stopping each event at the window before anything else hears it), so that nothing the answer is for can change; the wait ends with the answer, the connection changing, or a timeout. Then the count bounces and glows red, the shape is chosen, and sparks burst out of the tile's edges and fall away like a firework's (`UnlockFanfare`, on a canvas over the whole screen), the grid staying open for them. An answer that never comes, the connection having gone, lets the user ask again after a while. Too few dollars is said over the tile clicked, by a refusal's coach mark (see [ftue.md](../networking/ftue.md)).
  - Whoever wore a locked shape in a saved look when it went on sale holds it (`DBUserVersionMigration`).
- **Agents** hold, gain and spend alike, through tools of their own that send the same signals (see [agents.md](../networking/agents.md)).

## Dollar bills
A `DollarBill` is a non-persistent object in the `Collectible` category, whose cap is the most a room holds at once. Only the server adds, moves or removes one (`DollarBillUtil`, stepped from the socket flush). It has no collider, so nothing is stopped by it, and it stays out of raycasts, so no click or view is either. Single-player rooms have none, having no server-side room.

- **Who counts**: nothing happens to a room's bills with nobody in it. A ghost counts as nobody, as for helpers: no bill comes for one, makes way for one or is collected by one.
- **Spawning**: after a random wait, which starts once the room has space for one, somewhere a person in the room can walk to and out of everyone's reach, in the air over the floor. Only such places, since a bill nobody can reach would keep a slot of the cap for good.
- **Flights** (`DollarBillFlightUtil`): a path of waypoints, each with its speed, that passes through no block and ends lying on whatever is under it. It is worked out step by step from the wind on the bill: paper takes on a wind's speed almost at once, glides on out of it and swings down like a leaf. Whatever it runs into stops it and it drops, but it skids along a floor. `SetObjectPathSignal` says a whole flight at once, and each browser plays it out by itself with `ObjectPathUtil`, as the server does to keep the room's copy where it is. Whoever comes in during one is told the rest of it, right after the room.
  - **Gusts** come now and then to a bill lying still, each a random wind of its own, so that no two curves match: it may lift the bill or skid it, turn, or swing it round in an eddy or a rising whirl. Some are blows, several pushes in a row, which carry a bill much farther as one path. A gust points at nobody, and a curve that would bring a bill nearer to someone within or near their reach is dropped for another; with none left, it doesn't blow. The wait for the next gust starts as it lands.
  - **A fan's stream** takes over at once, in flight too (`PhysicsManager.getStrongestStream`, the strongest where streams overlap): along it to about the end of its reach or the first block, the stronger the faster. It lets go only where that path ends, so an upward fan tosses a bill and catches it again, and it never shudders at a stream's end.
  - **Nothing under it** (a block taken away, or just spawned): it flutters down.
- **Every browser agrees**: the server alone decides anything about a bill, streams included, and browsers do as told, in the order told. Told of a path far from where a bill is (back from the background), a browser starts it over from the path's start. How a bill rests needs no signal of its own (see Drawing).
- **Collecting** is decided by the server alone, at each flush, from where a player is and which way they face: a bill within `DOLLAR_BILL_REACH` across the room, about level with them, within 45 degrees of the way they face, ignoring height, and in sight of their eyes, on their screen or below it. The nearest of several takes it. The reach is past touching, since a flush or two pass before the server hears of a move.
  - A player's direction points behind them; browsers send it as it changes.
  - **In sight**: any of a few points of the bill with no block in between, by a walk through every cell the line crosses (`VoxelQueryUtil`), and no object drawn in the room, other players and helpers included, judged by their colliders (`PhysicsManager`). A volume, which draws nothing, hides nothing.
  - **Only by a player whose moves are believable** (`PlayerMotionCheckUtil`): a browser's reports are otherwise taken as told within its budgets, so a forged leap, run or hover could reach any bill. Going farther across the room than a walk would (more where a fan's stream reaches them), or rising or staying up in the air with nothing under them, keeps a player from collecting for a while: about as long as walking there would have taken, and more. A body pressed against a block it could step onto is held up by the physics for as long as it presses, which counts as held, though not as footing to rise from. Agents, whose bodies the server walks, pass it as browsers do.
  - The server counts it and removes it at once, telling the room who collected it just before (`ObjectCollectedSignal`), and a gain to them alone. What follows is each browser's to show. A bill that is only destroyed shows nothing.
- **Blocks**: one laid over a bill destroys it, with nobody the richer. Blocks aren't refused for a bill.
- **A room file** loaded over a room takes its bills with it, and the room's life goes on.

## Drawing
- Each half of the fold is an instance of a two-sided quad, its matrix written by the object: a fold that opens and shuts on a body that tumbles is more than a part's offset and facing can say. In a wind, which rises with each flight and dies down as it ends, it flaps and tumbles; lying still it writes nothing.
- It rests standing on its two edges, or tipped over with one half flat and the other a little open. Which, and which way it is turned, follow from its id and where it stops (`DollarBillPoseUtil`), both told alike to every browser, however it heard of them. Only its flapping in flight differs from screen to screen.
- Both sides show the photograph the right way round, a real bill's back being more than the law below allows and more bytes.
- **Collected**, a bill outlives its removal on every browser, one way on the collector's and another on everyone else's:
  - **On the collector's screen it flies straight to the count**, which sucks it up. It sets off from where it is on their screen, looking just as it did, and its way is planned on the screen rather than in the room, kept to the camera. From there on it is an instanced mesh of its own (`overRoom`), drawn over the room, unlit and unfogged: nothing should hide it, and the head light, above the camera, would blacken any face tilted down.
  - **On everyone else's it is drawn into the collector's body**, wherever that browser has it, over a fixed time: it leaps up, comes across faster and faster and shrinks away. "+$1" then shoots up out of them, staying under a storey's ceiling. A camera page sees this too, having no user of its own.
  - Leaving the room ends either at once.
- Being a picture of money, the texture is kept as the law on that asks as far as a game can: see `public/app/assets/resources/DollarBill/README.md`.
