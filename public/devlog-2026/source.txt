[Introducing ThingsPool, a 3D World You Can Rebuild] August 8, 2026

@@<h3>New here? Start with <a class="inlineButton" href="https://thingspool.net#what-is-thingspool">What is ThingsPool?</a></h3>

ThingsPool is an immersive 3D chat app that runs in a browser tab, with no install and no account required. You walk into a shared room as a character, alongside anyone else who opened the same link, and almost everything in that room is yours to change.

Rooms are built out of blocks. Swap a wall's material, cut a doorway through it, hang a painting on it and stretch the frame to whatever size you like — all of it from inside the room, while other people are walking around you.

<devlog-intro-editing>

The paintings come from a gallery of Monet, Van Gogh, Hopper, Pissarro and about twenty other artists. Each one is labeled with its title and its painter, which anyone standing in the room can read.

No room starts as an empty box. Every one is generated before you arrive, with its walls laid out and its first paintings already hung, so you begin with a furnished room rather than a blank grid. Whatever you change is what the next visitor walks into.

A room can become almost anything from there: a gallery of your favorite paintings, a maze to get lost in, or a house that you and a friend build together while standing inside it. Sign in and one room becomes permanently yours, to keep and to invite other people to build in.

This is the very first post of ThingsPool's development log, which is basically a news feed whose job is to share the major updates happening in this project.

[Game Modes Are Here] August 15, 2026

@@<h3>New here? Start with <a class="inlineButton" href="https://thingspool.net#what-is-thingspool">What is ThingsPool?</a></h3>

ThingsPool, an immersive 3D chat app that runs in a browser tab, now has game modes. Enter play mode to explore a room and talk to whoever else is in it, or switch to edit mode to craft the space you actually want.

Until now the two were one and the same. Clicking a painting to read its title also swung the camera around it and brought up the editing tools, so there was no way to simply look at a room.

Play mode is for exploring: a first-person adventure through rooms you have never seen, shared with whoever else walked in. Anything you click introduces itself — a painting gives you its title and its painter — and you keep walking.

<game-mode-play>

Edit mode is the opposite. Everything around you becomes yours to customize: your own avatar first — its shape and its colors, part by part, carried into every room you visit afterwards — and then the interior of the room itself.

<game-mode-character>*

Trade one painting for another, restyle its frame, stretch it across the wall, or take the wall out from behind it.

<game-mode-editing>

Separating the two modes also frees up the ordinary click. Picking an object up, eating it, repairing it, handing it to another player, swinging it at something — every one of those is a click on a thing, and none of them could exist while a click already meant "edit this".
