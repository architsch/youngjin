# Hashtags

A dev-log post is found by people who were not looking for it, and on LinkedIn the hashtag line is
part of how that happens. So the tags are not a summary of the post — a tag that describes the
*project* belongs on a post about one camera behaviour, even though the post itself says nothing
about virtual worlds or open source.

**About sixteen per post**, on one line at the end, each a `#` followed by a PascalCase term.

## Why sixteen and not fifty

The line is short on purpose, for three reasons that all point the same way:

- **Every tag is spent from the post's character budget.** A line of thirty tags runs to some 370
  characters — an eighth of everything LinkedIn allows — taken out of the prose that has to do the
  actual persuading. The tags help someone find the post; the paragraphs are what make them open
  the game.
- **The feed prefers a handful too.** Past a certain length the line reads as spam to a human
  scrolling past, whatever the algorithm makes of it. This is the reason that actually bites.
- **The same line sits on a public web page**, where hashtags earn nothing. Search engines read
  `#WebGL` as the word "WebGL" with a character stuck to the front of it; there is no hashtag index
  on thingspool.net for it to point at.

Note what is *not* among the reasons: search-engine risk. Google's keyword-stuffing policy is aimed
at pages whose substance *is* the keyword block — lists of phone numbers, blocks naming every city
a page wants to rank for, the same phrase repeated hundreds of times. A line of sixteen distinct
terms, each appearing once, under a human-written article is the ordinary blog tag list, and it
sits nowhere near that. Do not cut the line further in the belief that a crawler is counting.

So the count is not the axis that matters, and sixteen is not a line that must not be crossed by
one. Two things matter instead: the block stays a small share of the page — under about a tenth of
the post's text, which is what sixteen tags comes to on a post of ordinary length — and every term
on it is defensibly about the page. A short post therefore takes fewer tags, not the same sixteen.

## The bank

The broad tags come from the groups below. Take **six to eight** of them, spread across the groups
rather than drawn from whichever row is nearest to the post's subject, and vary the choice from
post to post — the bank is far bigger than one post's line, and rotating through it means each post
reaches a slightly different set of people instead of the same one over and over.

**The project** (always)
`#ThingsPool`

**The kind of place it is**
`#Metaverse` `#VirtualSpace` `#Cyberspace` `#Multiplayer` `#VirtualReality` `#VirtualWorld`
`#Chat` `#VR` `#Sandbox` `#OpenWorld`

**What it is built with, and how it is licensed**
`#HTML5` `#JavaScript` `#TypeScript` `#OpenSource` `#FreeToPlay`

**The kind of game it is**
`#IndieGame` `#SimulationGame` `#OnlineGame` `#BrowserGame` `#WebGame` `#MultiplayerGame`
`#SocialGame` `#MetaGame` `#SandboxGame`

**The craft of making it**
`#GameNarrative` `#GameDesign` `#GameDev` `#GameDevelopment` `#GameEngine` `#Gameplay` `#GameAI`
`#GameMechanics` `#GameplaySystems`

**The arts it is for**
`#VisualArts` `#VirtualArts` `#FineArts` `#Arts` `#NarrativeDesign` `#Multimedia` `#DigitalArt`
`#ArtExhibition`

**Everything adjacent**
`#EdTech` `#AdTech` `#Edutainment` `#WebApp` `#SocialNetworking` `#WorldBuilding` `#SeriousGames`
`#SoftwareToy` `#InteractiveApp` `#InteractiveMedium` `#Avatar` `#SocialApp`

## The rest of the line

The remaining seven or eight tags belong to *this* post, and they are the ones that make the set
worth having at all:

- **What the post is actually about** — `#CameraControl`, `#VoxelEngine`, `#ProceduralGeneration`,
  `#LevelEditor`, `#RealTimeMultiplayer`, `#UIDesign`.
- **Named technology**, where the post names it in the prose: `#ThreeJS`, `#WebGL`, `#SocketIO`,
  `#NodeJS`, `#React`.

## Rules

- PascalCase throughout, with acronyms and proper nouns keeping their own casing: `#WebGL`,
  `#UIDesign`, `#FTUE`, `#ThreeJS`.
- A tag need not match the post exactly — `#Edutainment` on a post about physics is reach, not a
  lie, since it describes the thing the post belongs to. But it has to be true of the project even
  where it is not true of the post, and at sixteen slots a stretched tag is taking one an accurate
  tag wanted, so keep it to a couple. A tag that describes neither the post nor the project is the
  one kind that genuinely reads as stuffing, on the page as much as in the feed.
- One line, at the very end, after every paragraph and image.
